# Recipient Onboarding

## Purpose

Make Geoffrey useful for the specific person receiving it without turning setup into a new project. The recipient should feel less load quickly. The onboarding packet is Geoffrey's working memory, not homework for the user.

## Product Frame

Do not frame Geoffrey as a clone of the old assistant. Frame Geoffrey as the new operating assistant that first handles what already matters, then quietly improves the way the work gets done.

Examples:

- Mauricio currently uses Viktor. Treat Viktor as Mauricio's current baseline, not the product vision.
- Another recipient may use ChatGPT, Claude, an executive assistant, a virtual assistant, a team member, or no assistant at all.
- If there is no current assistant, the baseline is the recipient's current manual workflow.

## Experience Standard

Optimize for a busy business owner who wants relief from load, mental fatigue, and process.

- Ask at most three questions at a time.
- Prefer "what is on your plate right now?" over abstract setup forms.
- Do useful work before producing a detailed map.
- Capture structure quietly in the background.
- Do not ask the recipient to design workflows, manage a rollout, or learn Geoffrey internals.
- Keep first outputs short, useful, and confidence-building.

## Low-Friction Start

Start with a short prompt such as:

```text
What are the top two things you rely on your assistant, team, or current process to handle, and what is one thing on your plate today that you would love not to carry?
```

If the recipient names a current assistant, ask:

```text
What does that assistant usually handle well, and where do you still feel the drag?
```

Then pick one real task that can be handled now. Do not require a full inventory before helping.

## Quiet Packet

As the conversation unfolds, maintain a simple internal packet with:

- Recipient profile.
- Current baseline.
- Load reducers: work that removes mental burden quickly.
- Must-match workflows.
- Better-than-baseline opportunities.
- Access needed.
- Approval boundaries.
- Preferences and standards.
- Next easiest tasks.
- Known gaps.

Only show the full packet when asked, at a handoff moment, or when it would reduce confusion.

## Baseline Capture

Learn the current baseline through examples and live work. For each recurring workflow, capture:

- Trigger: what starts the work.
- Inputs: messages, files, meetings, CRM records, notes, transcripts, links, or context.
- Output: what gets produced.
- Destination: where the output goes.
- Cadence: daily, weekly, monthly, event-based, or ad hoc.
- Quality bar: what makes the output useful.
- Authority: read-only, draft, update, send, publish, spend, delete, or automate.
- Risk: what could go wrong if Geoffrey does it poorly.

If the recipient is tired, skip the full list and ask for one example.

## First Useful Task

The first task should be real, low-risk, and immediately relieving.

Good first tasks:

- Draft a follow-up the recipient has been avoiding.
- Summarize a real thread, document, call, or messy note.
- Turn a live business worry into a short decision memo.
- Create a short priority list from what is currently in their head.
- Review a proposal, sales note, or client situation and recommend the next move.

Avoid first tasks that require sending, publishing, spending, deleting, or changing external records unless the recipient explicitly grants that authority.

## After The First Task

After completing the first task, report:

- The finished output.
- What Geoffrey learned about the recipient's work style.
- One thing Geoffrey can take off their plate next.
- Any access or approval needed, in plain language.

Do not present a long migration plan unless the user asks.

## Optional Workflow Snapshot

When a snapshot is useful, keep it compact:

| Work Geoffrey Can Help With | Current Baseline | Needs Anything? | Next Easy Win |
|---|---|---|---|

Use these readiness labels:

- Ready now
- Needs access
- Needs example
- Needs build
- Too risky for now

## Ready Definition

Geoffrey is ready for a recipient when:

1. The recipient can install Geoffrey or has someone install it once.
2. Geoffrey has completed one real useful task.
3. The recipient has accepted or corrected the output.
4. Geoffrey knows the highest-value current baseline workflows.
5. Approval boundaries for risky actions are clear enough to proceed safely.
6. The next easiest load-reducing task is identified.

If these are missing, say Geoffrey is still learning the recipient's world, then help with the next useful task.
