# Business Owner OSLO

## Purpose

Help a business owner find the constraint that matters now, choose one practical action, and avoid spreading effort across the wrong part of the business. Keep this as an optional business-owner pack; do not apply it to users who are not asking about a business, offer, pipeline, demand, fulfillment, or operating constraint.

## Core Sequence

Diagnose in this order:

1. Offers: what is sold, who it is for, the promised outcome, packaging, pricing, proof, and perceived value.
2. Sales: how qualified interest becomes revenue through follow-up, calls, proposals, objection handling, and close process.
3. Leads: how qualified demand is created through content, referrals, partnerships, paid channels, events, search, or other distribution.
4. Operations: how the business delivers, onboards, supports, bills, reports, retains, and scales after the sale.

Use the first weak pillar in the sequence as the primary bottleneck. Do not recommend scaling later pillars when an earlier pillar is clearly broken.

## Intake

Gather only enough information to diagnose the current constraint:

- Business model, offer, target customer, price range, and promised outcome.
- Current traction: revenue, active customers or clients, pipeline, conversion, churn, capacity, and recent trend if known.
- Current complaint: what feels stuck, noisy, slow, leaky, or dependent on the owner.
- Recent inputs: number of qualified conversations, calls, proposals, leads, campaigns, delivery issues, refunds, or support problems.
- Current tools and workflows only when they affect the bottleneck.

If numbers are unavailable, proceed with qualitative evidence and label the diagnosis as tentative.

## Scoring

Score each pillar from 0 to 5 using current evidence:

- 0: absent, or so unclear the owner cannot operate it.
- 1: exists only as an idea; owner cannot explain it crisply.
- 2: works occasionally but is inconsistent or owner-dependent.
- 3: works enough to operate, but has visible leaks or delays.
- 4: reliable enough to support the next pillar.
- 5: strong, measured, and repeatable.

A pillar with no evidence either way is unscored, not 0. Unscored means "not yet looked at." Zero means "looked at and found missing." Never name an unscored pillar as the bottleneck.

### Read Upstream Pillars From Downstream Activity

Most first messages describe one symptom, not four pillars. Before treating an earlier pillar as unscored, infer it from what the downstream activity already proves:

- Prospects are booking calls: Leads is working well enough to test the pillars after it. Score it 3 or higher unless volume is the stated complaint.
- Proposals are going out: Offers cleared the comprehension bar with real buyers. Score it 3 or higher unless the stall is about price, scope, or what exactly the buyer gets.
- Clients are being delivered and are renewing: Operations is 3 or higher.

Activity downstream is evidence upstream. A pillar that is visibly producing work for the next pillar is not the bottleneck, even when the owner never described it.

### Choosing The Bottleneck

Pick the earliest scored pillar under 4.

If an earlier pillar is still unscored after the inference above, and a later pillar shows a clear failure signal, do one of these. Do not default to the earlier pillar:

- Diagnose the later pillar, label it tentative, and name the earlier-pillar assumption it rests on.
- Ask one targeted question that would confirm or break that assumption.

If every scored pillar is 4 or higher, choose the pillar with the highest-leverage improvement for the next 30 days.

## Pillar Signals

Use these signals to reason about the diagnosis:

- Offers bottleneck: unclear buyer, vague outcome, weak proof, pricing resistance before trust exists, too many packages, or qualified prospects do not quickly understand the value.
- Sales bottleneck: people are interested but do not buy, follow-up is inconsistent, calls drift, proposals stall, objections repeat, or the owner must rescue every deal.
- Leads bottleneck: offer and sales work when prospects appear, but qualified conversations are too few, unpredictable, or over-dependent on one channel.
- Operations bottleneck: sales create delivery strain, onboarding is messy, quality varies, support rises, billing/reporting slips, clients churn, or fulfillment depends on the owner.

When signals conflict, explain the tradeoff and favor the earlier pillar unless later-pillar evidence is much stronger.

## Recommended Moves

Recommend one primary move, plus at most two supporting moves.

For Offers:

- Clarify the target customer and costly problem.
- Write the promised outcome in one plain sentence.
- Tighten the package, price logic, proof, guarantee, or qualification criteria.

For Sales:

- Define the sales path from qualified interest to decision.
- Prepare responses for the top objections using real buyer language.
- Add next-step discipline to every call, proposal, or follow-up.

For Leads:

- Choose one channel before adding more.
- Define the message theme, lead magnet, referral ask, event, ad, or distribution experiment.
- Set a small weekly target for qualified conversations, not vanity reach.

For Operations:

- Document one delivery process that currently lives in the owner's head.
- Fix onboarding, handoff, billing, reporting, support, or retention friction.
- Design one agent/workflow only after the process and owner are clear.

## Monthly Review

For recurring reviews:

1. Compare this month to the last known diagnosis.
2. Re-score Offers, Sales, Leads, and Operations.
3. Identify whether the bottleneck moved, stayed, or was misdiagnosed.
4. Choose one 30-day pillar play.
5. Record the decision, owner, due date, and evidence to check next time.

If the same pillar remains the bottleneck for three review cycles, recommend deeper investigation, outside help, or a smaller, more testable intervention.

## Output Format

Return a compact business diagnosis:

- Primary bottleneck: pillar and confidence.
- Evidence: the 3-5 signals that drove the diagnosis.
- What not to do yet: later-pillar work that would waste effort.
- One next move: a concrete action for the next 7-30 days.
- Review trigger: when and how to check whether the bottleneck moved.

Avoid generic strategy essays. Keep the recommendation specific enough that the owner can act this week.
